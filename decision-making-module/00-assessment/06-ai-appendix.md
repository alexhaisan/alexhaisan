> *"You may use generative AI in answering this question, but if you choose to do so, **you
> must include a list of the prompt you used as an appendix**. You do not need to include the

## 2. What the rubric actually rewards

AI Use is **one of three marking traits**, so roughly a third of the mark. It only applies if
you used AI, which you have.

| Band | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 40–49 | *"**Over reliance** on AI, with inadequate indications of understanding of the response generated"* |
| 50–59 | *"some demonstration of understanding… although response included **significant errors which were not corrected**"* |
| 60–69 | *"used in a **suitable manner**, with evidence of understanding… and **no significant errors**"* |
| **70–79** | *"AI used to **enhance communication** about the decision chosen, with **any errors in the generated text corrected**. **Strong evidence of understanding** of responses used"* |
| 80–100 | **word-for-word identical to 70–79** |

### Three things the top band wants
1. **AI enhancing communication** — helping you express your decision, not supplying it
2. **Errors in the generated text corrected** — you caught things and fixed them
3. **Strong evidence of understanding** — you knew what you were looking at

> ⚠️ The rubric's first trait carries the note *"Sections in italic apply where students have
> chosen to use generative AI."* Only the note itself is italicised in the file I was given,
> so I cannot tell which band descriptors it refers to. **Worth asking about**, since it may
> change how the other traits are marked for AI-assisted work.

## 3. What the appendix should look like

### The key insight: your prompts are the evidence

The brief asks for a list of prompts. You might think you then need to *explain* that you
engaged critically. **You don't.** Listing your actual prompts verbatim shows it, because
several of them are corrections:

> *"i dont want to use my lectureers own words i want to use his framework"*
> *"did you incoude rhis"*
> *"no i mean the general layout now exactly"*
> *"i did not ask actually this was an option i did not explore until i spoke to my parents"*

Those are not the prompts of someone pasting an answer in. A marker reading them sees
pushback, fact-checking and redirection without you having to claim any of it.

**So: list them straight. Don't annotate, don't editorialise.**

### Format

```
Appendix: Generative AI prompts

The following prompts were used with Claude (Anthropic) during the preparation
of this submission. Listed in order of use.

 1.  [prompt]
 2.  [prompt]
 3.  [prompt]
 ...
```

### Rules for the list
- **Verbatim.** Typos and all. Tidying the spelling is fine; changing what you asked is not.
- **In order.** It shows the work developing.
- **All of them**, or a clearly-labelled selection. If you group, say so: *"abridged; routine
  follow-ups omitted."* Don't silently drop the ones where you were asking for a draft.
- **Name the tool** and ideally the date range.
- Same formatting as the rest: Verdana 11pt, 1.5 spacing.

## 4. An honest point about scope

The rubric's top band describes AI *"used to enhance communication about the decision
chosen"*. That phrasing implies the thinking is yours and the AI helps you say it.

**Be straight with yourself about how far your use matches that.** In this session:

| Genuinely yours | Substantially mine |
|---|---|
| The decision, and the fact you withdrew | The drafting and most of the phrasing |
| Every fact: the offer, the salary, the contract, your reasons | The tax and opportunity-cost calculations |
| Spotting that the option set was wrong in an earlier draft | The analytical framing of the deviation |
| Catching that I claimed two things were included when they were not | The figures |
| Rejecting the lecturer's phrasing | |

That is a real and defensible pattern of use, and the corrections column is unusually strong.
But the appendix should describe it **accurately** rather than flatter it. If the honest
description of your use is broader than "enhancing communication", say so plainly and let the
marker weigh it. A list of prompts that shows heavy drafting use, declared openly, is a much
better position than one that implies light-touch help.

**This is a judgement only you can make**, and it is worth making before you submit rather
than after.
