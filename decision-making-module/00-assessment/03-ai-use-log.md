## 1. What the brief requires

> *"You may use generative AI in answering this question, but if you choose to do so, you must
> include a list of the prompt you used as an appendix. You do not need to include the AI's
> response to your prompt. This appendix does not count towards the 300-word limit."*

Three things follow directly from that sentence:

1. **AI use is permitted.** It is not a breach to use it.
2. **Declaration is mandatory, not optional**, if it is used.
3. What must be declared is **the prompts**, not the outputs.

## 2. Your stated plan, recorded

> *"we will use copilot ai to proof read our final submission — make sure we note this down
> as only copilot is allowed to do this"*

Recorded. Copilot is the tool you intend to use for the final proofread.

**One thing to check before submission, and it is worth checking properly:**

The brief's wording is *"generative AI ... in answering this question"* — which is broad.
This knowledge base, and any assistance used to develop, structure or refine the answer,
falls inside that scope on a plain reading. The safe and straightforward position is to
declare **every** generative AI tool used at any stage, including this one.

Two questions this raises that only the module handbook or module leader can settle:

- **Is the module restricted to an approved tool list?** If the university's policy is that
  Copilot is the only sanctioned tool for this module, that is a *tool permission* question
  and is separate from the *declaration* requirement. Both need to be satisfied.
- **Does the declaration cover preparatory work, or only text generation?** Some handbooks
  limit the appendix to prompts that produced submitted text; others cover all AI assistance.

Until you have confirmed, **log everything**. An over-complete appendix costs nothing (it is
outside the word count); an incomplete one is an academic integrity risk on a piece worth
20% of the module.

## 5. A line worth holding

The decision *you* write about in part B has to be genuinely yours — a real decision, with
real reasoning you can reconstruct. That is the one part of this assessment no tool can
supply, and it is also the part the marker can most easily tell apart. Everything in this
knowledge base is scaffolding for your own analysis of your own decision.
