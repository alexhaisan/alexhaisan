## 1. The rubric

Three traits, each banded 0-29 / 30-39 / 40-49 / 50-59 / 60-69 / 70-79 / 80-100.

### Trait 1 — Components of IRC
*"Demonstrates understanding of how components of IRC apply to real-world decision-making."*

| Band | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 40-49 | *"adequate comprehension of how IRC applies to chosen decision, although demonstrates **some confusion about components**"* |
| 50-59 | *"**reasonable** comprehension of the components… can **map them** to elements of the decision chosen. **Limited understanding of the complexities**"* |
| **60-69** | *"**good** comprehension… applies them **correctly**. Demonstrates understanding of **how components interact with each other** and feed into the decision-making process"* |
| **70-79** | *"**thorough** comprehension of IRC and **its complexities**. Can apply IRC to **complex decision-making scenarios**. Shows understanding of how **each component** interacts with the decision-making process"* |
| 80-100 | *"**outstanding** comprehension of predictions made by IRC in complex scenarios, demonstrates **reading/understanding of theory beyond what has been taught**"* |

| 80-100 | *"**outstanding** comprehension of weaknesses of IRC… **depth of understanding beyond the module**"* |

### Trait 1: why 62 and not 70

**Met for 60-69:**
- All four components mapped, each specific to the decision ✅
- No category errors. Action holds options, Preferences holds wants, Beliefs holds
  expectations, Evidence holds the basis ✅
- *"understanding of how components interact"* — one interaction stated explicitly ✅

**Not met for 70-79:**
- *"how **each** component interacts"* — we show **one** chain (Preferences → Evidence →
  Action). Nothing on Evidence → Expectations, nothing on the blocked Preferences →
  Expectations link.
- *"**complex** decision-making scenarios"* — a consumer purchase with five options is not
  complex, and the marker will see that. This is partly baked into the decision chosen.
- *"IRC and **its complexities**"* — we touch one complexity (the optimal-investment
  requirement) and stop there.

**Not met for 80+:** nothing beyond the module. The Elster citation is set reading.

### Trait 2: why 64 and not 70

**Met for 60-69, and met well:**
- *"Accurate assessment of **how well each component matches** observed decision-making"* —
  this is exactly what the elimination sentence does: *"My option set was complete and both
  expectations proved accurate, which isolates the gap at Evidence."* That line is the
  strongest thing in the draft against this rubric. ✅
- *"Strong evidence of observation"* — the one-week novelty claim and the breakdown of what
  kinds of evidence were consumed are specific and personal, not generic ✅

**Not met for 70-79 — and this is the single biggest cap on the whole piece:**
- *"thorough comprehension of **critiques of IRC**"*. The draft shows IRC failing to predict
  **Alex's behaviour**. It never says what that reveals about **the model**. There is no
  named weakness of IRC anywhere in 250 words.

That is a precise, fixable gap. See §4, Move 1.

### Trait 3: why it is currently unscoreable

The appendix does not exist yet. Written badly, this trait drops to 40-59 and drags the whole
mark down. Written well it is the **most achievable 70+** of the three, because Alex has real
evidence for the descriptor: *"any errors in the generated text corrected."*

He corrected a factual error in v1 (the claim that official Apple repair never entered the
option set). That correction changed the argument. It is exactly what the band asks for.

## 4. Four moves, ranked by marks per word

### Move 1 — Name a weakness of IRC itself · ~22 words · **Trait 2: 64 → 70-72**

The largest single gain available. The 70-79 band needs *"thorough comprehension of critiques
of IRC"*, and we currently offer none.

The best critique for this decision is **Elster's own**, which means it is set reading and
costs no extra credibility: the optimal investment in information gathering is
**indeterminate**.

> *"Whereas spending less time than the lower bound and more than the upper bound would be
> irrational, no choice the agent makes within this interval can be characterized as
> irrational."* — Elster (2007, pp. 208-209)

So IRC's third requirement cannot tell you **how much** to search. It can only catch you
searching in the wrong **place**. That is a real limitation of the model, named, and tied
directly to the component where the deviation sits.

### Move 2 — Add a second interaction · ~8 words net · **Trait 1: 62 → 66-68**

Show the **same** component feeding two expectations with different success. The marketing
material fixed the performance expectation accurately. It said nothing about duration, which
was the expectation that decided the case.

That is Evidence → Expectations, alongside the existing Preferences → Evidence. Two
interactions, not one, which moves toward *"how each component interacts."*

### Move 3 — Write the AI appendix properly · **Trait 3: unscoreable → 70+**

A full third of the rubric. Currently blank. Specification in
[`03-ai-use-log.md`](03-ai-use-log.md).

It must show engaged use and corrected errors, not a single "write my coursework" prompt.
Alex's actual history is unusually strong evidence for the top band.

### Move 4 — Accept the complexity ceiling · no word cost

Both traits reserve 70+ partly for *"complex decision-making scenarios"*. An iPhone purchase
is not complex, and no amount of writing makes it so. The honest response is to maximise the
*analytical* complexity surfaced within it, which Moves 1 and 2 do, and not to chase the
scenario descriptor.

**Chasing 80+ is not worth it.** Both top bands require *"theory beyond what has been
taught"*, which in 250 words would cost more than it returns.
