## The two assessments share one question

This matters more than it first appears.

**Slide 6 — the full question, attached to the FINAL 2,000-word essay:**

> *Define the Instrumental Rational Choice model and its components, and then describe a
> decision you made recently which does not follow this model, explaining how your
> decision-making process differed from this model. With reference to relevant literature,
> use a theory or experiment which we have studied on this module to explain your behaviour
> in this situation. How might you apply what you have learned in order to make better
> decisions in the future?*

**Slide 7 — the task attached to the INTERMEDIATE 300-word assessment:**

> *You will need to choose a decision you made recently where your decision **differs from**
> what an instrumental rational choice model would predict. Your task is to **analyse that
> decision using the instrumental rational choice model, applying the different elements of
> the model to the circumstances of your decision and identifying how an instrumentally
> rational decision-maker with your preferences would act in this situation, noting how that
> differed from your decision.***

### What this means

The intermediate piece is the **first two components** of the final essay question —
*define the model* and *apply it to your decision* — written at 300 words. The literature
and the "better decisions in future" sections belong to the December essay, where there is
room for them.

The 300-word piece is a **building block**, not a miniature of the essay. Choose a decision
you are willing to live with until 10 December, because you will almost certainly write the
2,000-word version about the same one.

## Module learning objectives (slide 5)

By the end of the module you should:
1. Understand what is meant by instrumental rationality, and be able to **apply an IRC model
   to a decision-making scenario**
2. Understand **how human decision-making may deviate from IRC**, and some theories which
   can explain this
3. Be able to **apply this knowledge to make better decisions**

These map one-to-one onto the three parts of the final essay question. The module is built
around the assessment.

## Week 1 lecture objectives (slide 3)

- Know the different elements of teaching on this module
- Understand how the module will be assessed
- Understand what is meant by Instrumental Rational Choice, and **the different elements of

> Note the wording: **"Elster's IRC model"**. The model being assessed is specifically
