# 04 — The Marking Criteria

> **Lecture 3, slide 17** is as close to a published mark scheme as this module gives you.
> It is headed *"Coursework Evaluation"* and was used to get students to assess a sample
> answer. Treat it as what the marker is looking for.

---

## The criteria, verbatim

> 1. *"How well has this student **mapped components** from IRC to their situation? Are there
>    any areas where they show a **lack of understanding of the component**?"*
>
> 2. *"Have they **correctly identified how their decision was irrational**? To what extent
>    have they used **components AND INTERACTIONS BETWEEN THEM** to do so?"*

---

## What each one means in practice

### Criterion 1 — mapping, and understanding what each component *is*

Two separate things are being judged:

| | What it rewards | What it penalises |
|---|---|---|
| **Mapping** | Each of the four components is visibly, specifically applied to *your* situation | A component left vague, generic, or missing |
| **Understanding** | You use each component the way the model defines it | Putting the right content under the wrong heading |

**The second half is where exemplars 1 and 2 lose marks.** Exemplar 1 files *"I believed my
grandmother would have preferred charity"* under **Beliefs** — but wanting to honour her
wishes is a **preference**. Exemplar 2 lists *"better camera quality, improved battery
health"* under **Preferences** — those are product attributes, i.e. **outcomes**.

Both show *"a lack of understanding of the component"* in the slide's exact words.

> **Check before submitting:** for each of your four rows, ask *"is this the kind of thing
> this component is?"*
> - Action = **options**, not reasons
> - Preferences = **what I want**, not what the thing has
> - Beliefs = **what I expect to happen**, not what I want to happen
> - Evidence = **what I based the belief on**, not the belief itself

### Criterion 2 — interactions between components

**This is the new, and the highest-value, requirement.** It is not enough to fill in four
boxes. The marker wants the **arrows**.

Elster's Figure 11.1 is a diagram of relationships, not a list:

| Interaction | What it looks like in your answer |
|---|---|
| **Evidence → Expectations** | "I had evidence X, from which I should have concluded Y, but concluded Z" |
| **Preferences ─╳→ Expectations** *(blocked)* | "I wanted X, so I believed X would happen" — **wishful thinking** |
| **Preferences → Evidence** | "Because I wanted X, I searched only where I expected to find support for it" |
| **Expectations → Evidence** | "Believing I already knew the answer, I stopped looking" |
| **Nothing → Preferences** | "My preferences shifted *because of* what I came to believe — the model has no arrow allowing that" |
| **Preferences + Expectations → Action** | "Given those preferences and that belief, the model predicts X; I did Y" |

> **Exemplar 4 is the only one of the four that does this**, and it is why it reads as the
> most sophisticated despite the worst writing. It names an Evidence→Beliefs failure
> (confirmation bias) *and* an illegal arrow into Preferences.

### How to signal an interaction in one sentence

> *"The failure is not in any single component but in the link between them: my preference
> for ___ shaped my expectation that ___, which Elster's model excludes — beliefs may be
> reached through evidence, never directly from desires (2007, p. 205)."*

---

## Practical consequences for the draft

1. **Four mapped components is the floor, not the ceiling.** Budget ~80 words for the
   Conclusion so there is room to discuss a relationship, not just label a box.
2. **Name one interaction explicitly.** It costs about 25 words and it is the single
   clearest way to hit criterion 2.
3. **Audit your headings for category errors** before submitting. That is criterion 1's
   second half and it is cheap to fix.
4. **"Correctly identified how their decision was irrational"** — the word *correctly*
   matters. A plausible-sounding but mis-located deviation scores worse than a modest,
   accurate one.

---

## Checklist

- [ ] All four components mapped, each specific to my situation
- [ ] Nothing filed under the wrong component (run the "is this the kind of thing…" test)
- [ ] **At least one interaction between components named explicitly**
- [ ] The deviation is *correctly* located, not just confidently asserted
- [ ] The Conclusion says which component shows the **biggest** difference (L2 slide 6)
