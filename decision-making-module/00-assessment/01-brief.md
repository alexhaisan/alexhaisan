> instrumentally rational decision-maker **with your preferences** would act in this situation,

## 5. Decoded — what is actually being marked

The question contains **four distinct instructions**. Each is a mark-bearing component.
Miss one and you cap your mark, no matter how good the other three are.

| # | Instruction | What the marker is looking for | Budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | *Define the IRC model and its components* | Correct, compact definition. **Named components**, not a vague gesture at "rationality". | ~70 w |
| **B** | *Describe a recent decision that does not follow this model, explaining how your process differed* | A real, specific, recent decision. The IRC **counterfactual** stated explicitly. The **gap** named. | ~80 w |
| **C** | *With reference to relevant literature, use a theory or experiment studied on this module to explain your behaviour* | A **module** theory (not a random pop-psych bias). A **Harvard citation**. A causal mechanism, not a label. | ~90 w |
| **D** | *How might you apply what you have learned to make better decisions in the future?* | A **specific, implementable** debiasing strategy tied to the mechanism in C. Not "I will think more carefully." | ~60 w |

Total ≈ 300. See `06-coursework-build/02-word-budget-and-structure.md` for the full plan.

## 6. Three traps hidden in the wording

### Trap 1 — "with your preferences"
This phrase does a lot of work. It means the counterfactual IRC agent **has the same
preferences you do**. So you *cannot* argue "I was irrational because I wanted a takeaway
instead of saving money." Wanting the takeaway *is* a preference, and IRC is silent on
the content of preferences — it only constrains their **structure** and the **process**
by which they are acted on.

**Your deviation must therefore be procedural, not preferential.** It must be a failure of:
- consistency (transitivity, invariance, independence), **or**
- information processing (belief updating, use of the right evidence), **or**
- means–ends alignment (you chose an action that did not best serve your own stated end).

The **Deviation Test** in `01-core-theory/04-irc-worked-template.md` screens for exactly this.
Run every candidate decision through it before you commit to writing about it.

### Trap 2 — "a theory or experiment which we have studied on this module"
Not *any* theory. A **module** theory. Until the course materials are uploaded, the candidate
list in `02-deviations-and-theories/` is provisional. Once the lecture slides are in
`04-course-materials/`, the first job is to **cross out every theory the module did not cover**.
Citing Prospect Theory beautifully will not help if the module taught Bounded Rationality
and the marker is working from a module-specific rubric.

### Trap 3 — "This exercise is not an essay"
Bullet points, tables and diagrams are **explicitly permitted**. This is a gift at 300 words:
- A two-column table (*IRC predicts* | *I actually did*) delivers component B at roughly
  half the word cost of prose.
- A small diagram (decision tree, value function, discounting curve) can carry part of
  component C. **Check whether figure labels count toward the word limit** — most
  institutions count all body text including table and figure text. Assume they do unless
  the handbook says otherwise.
