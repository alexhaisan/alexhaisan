## Three things worth taking

### ① The altruism/reciprocity distinction (CF2, CF3)
**Unconditional** vs **conditioned on what the other did**. L4 slide 14's phrase *"altruism,
unconditional kindness"* comes straight from CF3. Using the distinction precisely — rather
than calling everything "fairness" — is cheap and visibly informed.

### ② The benchmark defence of lab abstraction (CF4, CF5)
The best answer on the reading list to *"lab experiments aren't realistic"*. The stark
anonymous one-shot setup is **deliberately** unlifelike — it is the **zero point** against
which the effects of communication, repetition and identity are measured. You cannot measure
the effect of communication without a no-communication baseline.

> Pair this with Binmore's *"dirty test tubes"* (1999, p. F17) and Croson and Gächter's
> *"descriptively inaccurate experiments are useful as well"* (2010, p. 124). Three sources,
> one argument, from three directions.

### ③ Why cooperation decays (CF7, CF8)
Not because people **learn to free-ride** — because **conditional cooperators stop being
exploited**. Andreoni (1988), cited at p. 11 n.5, shows a surprise **restart** sends
contributions straight back up, which the learning account cannot explain.

> This is a lovely example of **evidence changing expectations**, which is exactly the IRC
> interaction the marking criteria reward: contributions fall because beliefs about others
> update, not because preferences change.
