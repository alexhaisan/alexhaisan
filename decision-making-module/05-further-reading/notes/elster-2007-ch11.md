## The quotations worth having

> **p. 191** — *"An action is rational, in this scheme, if it meets three optimality
> requirements: the action must be optimal, given the beliefs; the beliefs must be as well
> supported as possible, given the evidence; and the evidence must result from an optimal
> investment in information gathering."*

> **p. 191** — *"The arrows have a double interpretation, in terms of causality as well as of
> optimality. The action, for instance, should be caused by the beliefs that make it a
> rational one; it is not enough to do the right thing by fluke."*

> **p. 193** — *"There is no implication that the desires be selfish. The confusion of
> rationality and egoism is a crude error."*

> **p. 193** — *"An agent who under the influence of emotion or drugs prefers A to B acts
> rationally in choosing A, even if she under other circumstances prefers B to A."*

> **p. 194** — *"A lack of completeness is not any kind of failure... reflects simply a fact
> of life."*

> **p. 202** — *"Rational beliefs are those that are shaped by processing the available
> evidence using procedures that, in the long run and on average, are most likely to yield
> true beliefs."*

> **p. 205 n.12** — *"Wishful thinking, in which 'the wish is the father of the thought,' is
> clearly irrational. By contrast, there is nothing irrational about the process shown in
> Figure 11.1, in which the desires are, as it were, the grandfather of the beliefs."*

> **p. 206** — *"We may view the gathering of information as a shadow action that accompanies
> the primary action."*

> **p. 209** — *"Consequences of a decision cannot explain it. Only the mental states that
> precede the decision enable us to explain the actions as optimal from the point of view of
> the agent rather than to characterize them as useful or beneficial from the point of view
> of an external observer (or of the agent at a later time)."*

> **p. 211** — *"The rationality of beliefs is a completely different matter from that of
> their truth. Whereas truth is a feature of the relation between the belief and the world,
> rationality is a feature of the relation between the belief and the evidence possessed by
> the agent."*

## Examples and devices

| Device | Page | Point it makes |
|---|---|---|
| **Smokers' cancelling biases** | 192 | Right belief by two wrong routes ≠ rational belief |
| **Weber's error** | 192 | Deviation from rationality is *sufficient* for irrationality, not *necessary* |
| **Stick or snake** | 192 | Reflexes are *adaptive*, mimicking rationality without being rational |
| **Money pump / "counting aspects" apples** | 193–194 | Intransitivity is exploitable: price/taste/perishability cycling |
| **Ice cream for two children** | 194 | Incompleteness is a fact of life, not a failure |
| **Lexicographic voter** | 195 | Sacred values resist trade-off; break continuity |
| **PM competence (60%→80%→91%→97%)** | 203–204 | Bayesian updating; later evidence matters less than earlier |
| **Cancer screening ($49k → $47m per case)** | 206 | Information has sharply diminishing returns |
| **Berry-picking** | 206, 208 | Optimal search depends on the search's own results (the self-loop) |
| **Germany invading France, 1940** | 206 | Shadow action and primary action can coincide — you learn enemy morale only by fighting |
| **Five car models A–E** | 207 | Incomplete preferences → indeterminacy |
| **Dr. Johnson on deliberation** | 208 | *"Life is not long, and too much of it must not pass in idle deliberation"* |
| **The discounting pill** | 209–210 | A rational agent refuses it — the behaviour is already within reach |
| **Disulfiram in Poland; the myopic man's glasses** | 211 | **Belief traps** — a false belief is rational to hold if testing it is believed too costly |

## Why this matters for the assessment

1. **Figure 11.1 is the model being assessed.** Reproducing it (or its logic) and mapping
   your decision onto it is literally the task.
2. **The three optimality requirements localise your deviation.** Name which one failed.
3. **p. 209 kills the two commonest wrong answers**: "it turned out badly" and "I regret it
   now". Both are the view of *"an external observer, or of the agent at a later time"*, and
   neither explains the decision.
4. **p. 193 kills the third**: "I was selfish/emotional/drunk" is not irrationality.
5. **Indeterminacy gives you evaluation material** that most students will not have: the
   model can fail by *under-determining* behaviour, quite apart from people being irrational.
