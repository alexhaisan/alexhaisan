## The spine of the chapter: three senses of rationality

| Sense | Rational action is… | Picture of the person |
|---|---|---|
| **Instrumental** | choosing the means that **best satisfy given objectives** | Well-defined objectives; reason assesses only *consistency*, not *content* |
| **Procedural** | following **rules, roles or norms** | *Homo sociologicus* — socially and historically located |
| **Expressive** | **deliberating on and creating the ends** themselves | Self-reflexive; "making sense of the self" |

> *"The picture of individuals which corresponds to these alternative senses of rationality is
> more open-ended than the instrumental one. People are less certain about their objectives
> and the environment in which they operate, **less autonomous but more active and enquiring**."*

**Conclusion (p. 24–25):** *"They should not be regarded as competitors: rather, they provide
complementary insights into human motivation which can be called on in different measures,
according to the setting."*

## Instrumental rationality, formally (pp. 4–11)

> *"Reason can assess only their **consistency**, not their **content**, since it is cast
> solely as the 'slave of the passions,' in Hume's (1740) words."*

**Axioms under certainty** (box, p. 6): 1 Reflexivity · 2 Completeness · 3 Transitivity ·
4 Continuity.
- (1)–(3) give a **preference ordering**; (1)–(4) allow representation by a utility function
  **unique up to a positive monotonic transformation**
- Transitivity defended by the **money pump**: an intransitive agent can be *"traded into
  poverty"*, and *"would — with the same set of preferences — be willing to pay again for the
  same cycle of trades, and so on into poverty"*
- Continuity rules out **lexicographic** orderings

**Axioms under uncertainty** (box, p. 9): 5 Preference ordering over prospects · 6 Preference
increasing with probability · 7 Continuity (ii) · 8 **Strong independence** · 9 Usual rules
for combining probabilities → **expected utility maximisation**, with a **cardinal** utility
function unique up to a **linear** transformation.

- The desires can be *"'good', 'bad', 'selfish', 'altruistic' — anything you like"*. Only the
  **ordering** is constrained. (Same point as Elster p. 193, arrived at differently.)
- **Arrow's continuity objection** (p. 10): I = $1 no risk to life; II = nothing, no risk;
  III = $1 with a small risk to life. Continuity demands III ≻ II if the probability of death
  is small enough — *"and this may strike some as surprising"*. But we cross the road to buy
  a soft drink.

### IRC can generate sophisticated behaviour (p. 12)
Two examples that pre-empt "but rational choice is simplistic":
- **Reputation:** a teacher penalises a late assignment, though waiving it maximises utility
  *now*, because a reputation for toughness *"may maximize utility **over time**"*
- **Ulysses and the mast:** tying yourself to the mast is uncomfortable at the time but
  enables the later listening — *"such a strategy is now referred to as **'self-command'**"*

## The Limits of Instrumental Rationality (pp. 14–17)

### Empirical
1. **Precise:** EU maximisation yields predictions *"which have been consistently falsified"*
   in experiments
2. **General:** many human projects simply don't fit. **States that are essentially
   by-products** (Elster, 1983) — *"imagine trying to satisfy the objective of 'going to
   sleep' or 'being spontaneous'. As any insomniac will tell you, to attempt to 'go to sleep'
   is a recipe for failure... **the moment you realise that the only reason you are taking
   this course of action is as a device for going to sleep, the strategy collapses.**"*
3. **Contradictory desires** — John Donne's *The Prohibition* quoted in full
4. **Preferences change over time** → which preferences should a plan satisfy?

> **MULTIPLE SELVES box (p. 16).** Bismarck: *"Faust complained that he had two souls in his
> breast. I have a whole squabbling crowd. It goes on as in a Republic."*
> **Becker & Stigler (1977)** reply that apparent preference change is really stable
> preferences plus changing shadow prices — advertising supplies *information*, the music
> lover's appreciation becomes cheaper with skill. Hargreaves-Heap's objection is sharp:
> *"Their individuals do not experience any inner conflict over what to do... He or she does
> not experience the symptomatic difficulty, for instance, of how to weigh the pleasure of
> another drink now with the regret which will follow it in the morning!"*

### Theoretical — the information regress (pp. 15–17)
Bayes tells you how to **update** beliefs, but how do you decide how much information to
**acquire**? The obvious answer — invest until marginal benefit = marginal cost — **begs the
question**:

> *"How is the individual to know what the marginal benefits of further information
> acquisition are without knowledge of the full information set? After all, from a point just
> below the summit of the foothills, there may seem little advantage in climbing to the top.
> Yet an aerial photograph would reveal that the tops of the mountains which lie beyond the
> foothills afford unimaginably better views."*

Appealing to subjective beliefs about those benefits only *"begs the question again at one
stage higher in the structure of beliefs"* — an infinite regress. **Rational expectations**
was hoped to solve it (errors only white noise) but *"a variety of expectations... could
satisfy the condition"*, so it does not determine *which* expectation to hold.

> This is the single strongest *internal* critique of IRC on the reading list — it attacks
> Elster's third optimality requirement from inside the model, not from psychology.

## Procedural rationality (pp. 17–20)

- **Simon (1978):** rules of thumb as short-cut devices economising on *"the brain's limited
  computational capacity"* — adaptive expectations; the payback-period investment rule
- In Simon's hands *"procedural rationality is really an **ersatz form of instrumental
  rationality**"* — satisficing rather than optimising, a bit of *"grit in the system"*
- **But that undersells it.** Shared rules are **irreducible**: *"give way to traffic coming
  from the right"* is not a second-best substitute for computation — *"No amount of
  computational power will enable you to anticipate what other people will do when their
  behaviour depends on what they expect of you: the circle of expectations merely becomes
  wider the more you calculate."* The shared rule is what converts **uncertainty** into
  something expectations can be formed about
- And rules **distribute**: *"Is it women who always give way to men, the old to the young,
  workers to capitalists...?"* The choice of convention is not neutral
- Marx: *"Men make their own history, but they do not make it just as they please; they do
  not make it under circumstances chosen by themselves"*
- Rule-following is not mechanical — *"all rules provide more or less space for creative
  interpretation"* (judges interpreting statute)

## Expressive rationality (pp. 21–24)

- Kant, Weber (***Wertrationalität***), Habermas (communicative action), Elster's broad
  notion. Weber's **four** types of social action: instrumentally rational
  [*zweckrational*], value-rational [*wertrational*], affectual, traditional
- Elster (1983, p. 15): *"between the thin theory of the rational and the full theory of the
  true and the good there is room and need for a **broad theory of the rational**... the
  requirement of consistency is too weak, that of ethical goodness too strong"*
- **"Making sense of the self"** cannot be folded back into instrumental rationality:
  *"It is more akin to a project which undermines the very notion of fixed 'ends' which are
  necessary for instrumental calculation."*
- **'Judgement'** is the contrast term — *"a stab at representing what we regard as worthy...
  often reduced to a messy and arbitrary kind of 'muddling through'"*
- 🔑 **The payoff line for your coursework (p. 24):**
  *"**Inconsistent objectives need not be taken as evidence of irrationality.** Rather, they
  might be regarded as marking our expressive rationality, although care is required here not
  to overstate the case, lest any kind of preference structure is thereby admitted!"*
