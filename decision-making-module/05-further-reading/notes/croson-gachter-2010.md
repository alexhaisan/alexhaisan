## What it is

Two practising experimental economists on the relationship between economic theory and
experiments. Deliberately even-handed — a useful counterweight to Binmore's partisanship.

> *"Our eclectic views in this essay are those of two 'practitioners' of experimental
> economics, not those of professional methodologists. Like most economists we are normally
> busy doing research, not philosophy of science."* — p. 122, §1, ¶2

## Three ideas worth lifting

### ① "Models are, by definition, wrong… this does not mean they are useless" (CG3, p. 123)

**The single most useful sentence in the paper for your coursework.** Your assessment asks
you to show IRC failing descriptively. CG3 and CG4 let you do that *without* implying the
model is worthless — IRC remains the **benchmark** against which the deviation is even
measurable. Pair with L1 slide 17: *"The model is a simplification — it doesn't always work
well. This is useful."*

### ② Expected utility can explain almost anything (CG7, p. 128)

A sharper version of the standard critique: the problem is not that EU is false but that it
is hard to falsify, because a utility function can always be constructed after the fact to
rationalise what was observed. Note the escape hatch they give: *"very few sets of
observations will falsify expected utility theory (violations of transitivity, preference
reversals, or violations of the independence axiom are in this small set)."*

> This connects straight back to `../../01-core-theory/02-preference-axioms.md`: the axioms
> matter precisely because they are the few things that *can* be violated.

### ③ The scope–accuracy trade-off (CG8 and Fig. 1, pp. 128–129)

Figure 1 plots a production possibility frontier with **Scope** on the vertical axis and
**Accuracy** on the horizontal, running from *Expected utility (own-profit maximisation)* at
the top left, through Bolton (1991), Bolton/Ockenfels (2000) and Fehr/Schmidt (1999),
Rabin (1993), **Kahneman and Tversky (1979) Prospect Theory**, down to **Psychology** at the
bottom right.

The point: IRC is **parsimonious but inaccurate**; psychology is **accurate but narrow**.
Behavioural models sit between. *"Theories with large scopes are parsimonious; they have
relatively few parameters."* (p. 128)

> This is an elegant way to end a December essay. The choice between IRC and a behavioural
> alternative isn't right-vs-wrong; it's a position on a frontier.

## The "Ten Commandments" (§7, pp. 129–130)

Addressed to experimentalists and theorists. The ones relevant to you:

> **#10** *"Experimentalists shall not Hypothesize After the Results are Known (HARK; see Kerr, 1998)."*
> **#7** *"Experimentalists shall replicate and encourage replications, including making your data, instructions and software publically available."*
> **#4** *"Theorists shall respect optimization and equilibria—not because they are true (as quite a lot of research on bounded rationality has shown), but because they are unique to economics and should not be lightly put aside."*
> **#2** *"All economic scientists (theory, experimental or empirical) shall not oversell their results."*
> **#1** *"All areas of science (economics included) need to be tolerant of multiple methodologies."*

> **#10 (HARK) has a sting for this coursework.** You are reconstructing a decision you
> already know the outcome of, then fitting a model to it. That is hypothesising after the
> results are known. It doesn't invalidate the exercise — but naming the risk in one clause
> in December would be unusually self-aware.

## How it pairs with Binmore

| | Binmore (1999) | Croson and Gächter (2010) |
|---|---|---|
| Posture | Combative — defends theory against debunkers | Even-handed — "gains from trade" between methods |
| On bad experiments | *"dirty test tubes"* (p. F17) | *"descriptively inaccurate experiments are useful as well"* (p. 124) |
| On method pluralism | Economists should run their own | *"tolerant of multiple methodologies"* (p. 130) |
| Best used for | A sharp, quotable critique | The balanced framing, and the benchmark defence of IRC |

Citing **both** — the attack and the measured reply — is worth more than either alone.
