## Where the top marks sit

Reading the outcomes together, three capabilities are weighted most heavily and are all
live in this piece:

1. **Applying core economic theory to an applied topic** — the stated central aim
2. **Evaluation**, not just description — judging the models, not reciting them
3. **Self-analysis** with a genuine developmental conclusion

### What that means in practice

| Register | Reads as |
|---|---|
| ❌ Descriptive | "IRC says people maximise utility. I didn't. This is called present bias." |
| ✅ Analytical | "IRC predicted X because my stated preference ranked X above Y; I chose Y, violating means–ends consistency at the moment of choice." |
| ✅✅ Evaluative | "IRC remains the right *normative* benchmark — my own later regret confirms its standard — but it mis-predicts *descriptively* here, because it assumes a time-consistent discount function the behavioural evidence contradicts." |

The third register is what separates a strong mark from a safe one. It costs about fifteen
extra words.
