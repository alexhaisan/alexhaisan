# 02 — Word Budget & Structure

> **300 words. This is the control document.** At this length, structure *is* the argument.

---

## The allocation

| Section | Component | Words | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Define IRC + components | **65–75** | Must *name* components. Bullets. No preamble, no "since the dawn of economics". |
| **B** | My decision vs. IRC counterfactual | **75–85** | Use a **two-column table** — roughly half the word cost of prose. |
| **C** | Theory + literature | **85–95** | The highest-credit section. One theory, one citation, one **mechanism**. |
| **D** | Future application | **55–65** | Specific and implementable. No platitudes. |
| | **Total** | **≈ 300** | |

> References sit outside the body. **Confirm whether the reference list counts** toward the
> 300 (see `../00-assessment/02-format-and-submission.md`).

## The skeleton

```
A. The Instrumental Rational Choice model
   One-line definition (means–ends, preferences given)
   • Agent  • Preferences (complete, transitive)  • Beliefs (Bayesian)
   • Constraints  • Optimisation  • Consequentialism

B. My decision
   One sentence: what I chose, when.

   | IRC agent with my preferences would… | I actually… |
   |--------------------------------------|-------------|
   | [counterfactual action]              | [actual]    |
   | [belief the evidence supported]      | [belief held]|

   The gap: [one precise sentence naming the violated component/axiom]

C. Explanation
   [Theory name] (Author, Year) explains this: [MECHANISM — why the theory
   predicts exactly this behaviour, not just a label for it]
   [Optional: the supporting experiment, in one clause]

D. Applying this
   [Specific architectural change] — because [links back to the mechanism in C].
   [Second concrete measure, if words allow]

References
Appendix 1: Generative AI prompts used
```

## Word-saving techniques that actually work

| Technique | Saving |
|---|---|
| **Table for component B** | ~30 words vs. prose |
| Cut every "In this essay I will…" / "It is important to note that…" | 10–20 words |
| Cut hedges: "arguably", "it could be said", "to some extent", "quite" | 10–15 words |
| Active voice: "I chose X" not "X was chosen by me" | 1–2 per instance |
| Name the theory once, then use "this" | 5–10 words |
| Bullets with no leading verb ("Preferences: complete and transitive") | 15–20 words |
| A small diagram carrying part of C | varies — **but check whether labels count** |

## Words not to save on

- The **gap sentence** in B. It is the hinge of the whole piece.
- The **mechanism** in C. "Present bias caused it" is a label; the marks are in the *because*.
- The **specificity** of D. A vague fix reads as not having understood the mechanism.

## Drafting sequence

1. Complete `../01-core-theory/04-irc-worked-template.md` for the chosen decision
2. Draft **without** a word limit — get the reasoning right first
3. Cut to 400
4. Cut to 320
5. Cut to 300 — at this stage, cut *adjectives and connectives*, never *content*
6. Read aloud: does each of A/B/C/D still visibly answer its question?
7. Format (Verdana 11, 1.5, 2.5cm) — see `../00-assessment/02-format-and-submission.md`
8. References + AI appendix
9. Proofread
10. Final checklist in `../00-assessment/02-format-and-submission.md`

## Self-assessment before submitting

Ask of the draft:

1. Does component A **name** the components, or just gesture at "rationality"? 
2. Does component B hold **my preferences constant** in the counterfactual?
3. Does component C explain a **mechanism**, or just attach a label?
4. Is the theory in C definitely **on the module**?
5. Is component D something I could **actually do on Monday morning**?
6. Could a marker find all four components **without hunting**?

If any answer is no, that is where the next revision goes.
