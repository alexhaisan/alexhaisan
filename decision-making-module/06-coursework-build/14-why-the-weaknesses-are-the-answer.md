# 14 — Why the weaknesses *are* the answer

> You asked whether including them improves the grade, or just shows reflection.
> It is neither. **They are the thing being marked.**

---

## The task, read literally

> *"Describe a decision you made recently which **does not follow** this model, explaining
> **how your decision-making process differed** from that model."*

Strip the weaknesses out and there is no difference to describe. The piece would say: here is
a decision, here are four components, the end. That is not an answer to the question asked.

The rubric says the same thing twice:

| Trait | What it asks for |
|---|---|
| Components of IRC | *"how components of IRC apply to real-world decision-making"* |
| Critical Engagement | *"understanding of weaknesses within IRC, and **why it fails to predict behaviour**"* |

**"Why it fails to predict behaviour" is your three weaknesses.** Without them the second
trait, a third of the mark, has nothing to assess.

---

## So the real question is not whether to include them

It is **how**. And here the difference is large.

| Confession — scores low | Analysis — scores high |
|---|---|
| "I was stupid and didn't check the numbers." | "Wanting the job settled which questions felt worth asking, and the sums were not among them." |
| "I let the sunk cost get to me." | "I counted three years of tuition already paid, which is spent whichever way I choose." |
| "I should have asked about deferring." | "I was on the phone to the hiring manager and never asked whether they would hold the role." |

Same three facts. The left column is an apology and describes. The right column gives a
**mechanism** and analyses.

**The marker is not grading your decision. They are grading whether you can take a real
decision apart with a model.** Being wrong is the raw material. Showing *how* the wrongness
worked is the skill.

---

## What each weakness is worth

| Your weakness | Which part of the model it breaks | Why it earns marks |
|---|---|---|
| **Stopped at the headline** | The link from what you wanted to what you went and found out | It is not "I was lazy". Wanting the job shaped which question you asked, so the search could only ever confirm. That is one component acting on another. |
| **Counted money already spent** | The model values actions only by what follows from them | Three years of fees land on both options equally, so they cannot separate them. This is the cleanest single break in the whole decision, and it needs no judgement call. |
| **Saw two options when there were three** | The requirement that every option be on the table | Strongest of the three, because the person who could have answered was already on the call. Nothing was hidden. |

Three weaknesses, three different parts of the model, and one of them shows two parts
interacting. That is exactly the spread the higher bands ask for.

---

## The one that makes the piece unusual

Most of your cohort will write: *I did something daft, here is the bias, I will think harder
next time.*

Yours says: **the decision was right, and the reasoning still failed.** Those are separate
things. You withdrew for reasons that do not hold up — a number wrong by half, money already
gone, an option you had not thought of — and the reasons that would have justified
withdrawing you only reached later.

That is harder to see and much harder to argue, and it is why the weaknesses need to be in
full rather than softened.

---

## What this does not mean

**It does not mean beating yourself up.** The tone should stay flat and factual. No
apologising, no "foolishly", no "I deeply regret". State what happened, state what the model
requires, show the gap. Four exemplars and both samples do it that way.

**It does not mean listing every flaw.** Three is right. A fourth would cost words the
analysis needs and would start to read as padding.
