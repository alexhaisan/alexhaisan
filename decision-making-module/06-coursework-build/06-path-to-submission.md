**Deadline: 26 October, 3pm, via Moodle.** Today is 22 October. Four days.

> Everything that can be built without your decision has been built. What follows is the
> sequence from here, with the blocking step first and an honest estimate of where the time
> actually goes.

## The seven steps

### 1. Choose the decision ⬅️ **BLOCKING**
**~20 minutes.** Answer the six questions in
[`05-purchase-decision-worksheet.md`](05-purchase-decision-worksheet.md).

Then screen it:
- Does it survive the Deviation Test? ([`01-decision-candidates.md`](01-decision-candidates.md))
- Does it avoid *"I went against my preferences"*? (L2 slide 5)
- Can you name **one** component with the biggest gap? (L2 slide 6)

If any answer is no, pick a different decision. **Nothing downstream can fix a weak choice
here.** This step carries more of the mark than any other.

### 2. Map the four components
**~30 minutes.** Use [`../01-core-theory/04-irc-worked-template.md`](../01-core-theory/04-irc-worked-template.md).

Fill in more than you'll need. Cutting is easier than expanding, and the material you cut
feeds the December essay.

Check each row against the category test in
[`../00-assessment/04-marking-criteria.md`](../00-assessment/04-marking-criteria.md):
Action = options. Preferences = what I want. Beliefs = what I expect. Evidence = what the
belief rests on.

### 3. Identify the interaction
**~15 minutes, and worth more per minute than anything else.**

L3 slide 17 asks for *"components **and interactions between them**."* Pick one link and
state what went wrong across it:
- Evidence → Expectations (I had X, concluded Y)
- Preferences ─╳→ Expectations (wishful thinking)
- Preferences → Evidence (I searched where I'd like the answer)
- Nothing → Preferences (my wants reshaped by what I believed)

### 4. Draft long
**~45 minutes.** Write it with **no word limit**. Get the reasoning right while you still
have room to be clumsy. Expect 450–600 words.

### 5. Cut to 300
**~30 minutes.** 600 → 400 → 320 → 300.

Cut in this order: filler phrases, hedges, adjectives, connectives, then whole clauses that
restate. **Never cut the counterfactual sentence, the named component, or the interaction.**

Target **230–270** words, not 299. The exemplars run 166–258, and a tight piece reads more
confidently than a padded one.

### 6. The prose pass
**~30 minutes.** [`07-prose-and-style.md`](07-prose-and-style.md).

Sentence rhythm, British spellings, repeated words, AI tells. Read it aloud. This is the step
most people skip and it is visible in exemplar 4, which has the best analysis and the worst
writing in the set.

### 7. Format, reference, submit
**~20 minutes.**
- Verdana 11pt, 1.5 spacing, 2.5cm margins, applied to the **whole document**
- Harvard reference if you cite ([`../07-references/01-harvard-style-guide.md`](../07-references/01-harvard-style-guide.md))
- AI appendix ([`../00-assessment/03-ai-use-log.md`](../00-assessment/03-ai-use-log.md))
- Final checklist ([`../00-assessment/02-format-and-submission.md`](../00-assessment/02-format-and-submission.md))
- Submit to Moodle **before** 3pm, not at 3pm

**Total: about three hours of real work.** The thinking in steps 1 and 3 is what matters; the
rest is execution.

## What "good" looks like, in one table

| Level | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Pass** | Four components mapped to a real decision. IRC's prediction stated. A difference noted. |
| **Solid** | Mapping is accurate — nothing filed under the wrong component. One component named as the biggest gap. The mechanism named, not just asserted. |
| **Strong** | All of that, plus **one interaction between components** analysed, and a counterfactual that holds preferences genuinely constant. |
| **Excellent** | All of that, written in prose that varies, with a verified citation used because it earns its place, and a single clause acknowledging that IRC remains the right benchmark. |

The gap between *solid* and *strong* is about 25 words. That is the best-value sentence in
the piece.

it, so keep the long draft. Weeks 2–4 then add:
- The literature requirement — see [`../07-references/03-quote-ledger.md`](../07-references/03-quote-ledger.md)
