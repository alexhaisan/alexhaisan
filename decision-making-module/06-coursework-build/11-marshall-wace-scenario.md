### 🔑 Finding 1 — the £82,000 gap is really £37,898

> Headline difference: **£120,000 − £38,000 = £82,000.**
> After tax, NI and student loan: **£37,898.**
> **54% of the apparent gap never reaches you.**

### 🔑 Finding 2 — the £40,000 bonus delivers £15,600

Because £100,000–£125,140 is where the personal allowance tapers at £1 for every £2 earned,
the marginal rate in that band is:

| Component | Rate |
|---|---|
| Higher-rate income tax | 40% |
| Personal allowance withdrawal | +20% |
| National Insurance | +2% |
| Student loan Plan 5 | +9% |
| **Marginal deduction** | **71%** |

> On the £40,000 bonus you keep **£15,600**, or **39 pence in the pound.**

This is the single most quotable number available, and almost nobody who takes a £120k offer
at 21 has run it.

## 3. Sunk cost vs forward-looking cost

**This is the heart of the IRC analysis, and it is where the instinctive reasoning fails.**

Alex's own words: *"all the money gone to the uni degree would have been wasted"* and
*"that's honestly a lot of wasted time and hard work and dedication."*

That is **sunk cost reasoning**, and IRC forbids it. L1 slide 14: instrumental rationality
means *"individuals only look at the outcome… ends always justify means (in fact, means don't
matter at all)."* Money already spent is not an outcome of this choice. It is spent either way.

| Item | Status | Decision-relevant? |
|---|---|---|
| ~£27,000 tuition for years 1–3 | **Sunk** | ❌ No |
| Three years of effort | **Sunk** | ❌ No |
| Year 4 tuition, ~£9,000 | Still payable | ✅ Yes |
| 8 months of forgone MW earnings | Still avoidable | ✅ Yes |
| ACA qualification | Asset acquired | ✅ Yes |
| The PwC job offer | Option acquired | ✅ Yes |
| The degree itself | Credential acquired | ✅ Yes |

**The correct forward-looking question is not** *"will £36,000 be wasted?"* **It is:**

> *"Does the remaining investment — roughly £9,000 of tuition plus about £28,000 net of
> forgone MW earnings over eight months, so call it £37,000 — buy something worth more than
> £37,000?"*

What it buys: the ACA, the degree, and a job offer. The ACA is **portable**; the MW role is
*"very niche"*. At 21, that portability has real option value.

> Note the asymmetry Alex has already half-spotted. Finish, and he holds the ACA, the degree
> and a PwC offer, while hedge-fund roles continue to exist. Take MW now and it fails, and he
> holds **none of them**.

## 4. The multi-year view, and discounting

Five-year cumulative **net** income, MW flat at £120k against a PwC progression of
£38k → £48k → £52k → £58k → £66k:

| | 5-year net |
|---|---|
| Marshall Wace | £338,037 |
| PwC path | £190,381 |
| **Gap** | **£147,656** |

Invested at 5% real, the annual surplus is worth about **£165,000** by year five. Discounted
back to today at the same rate, the NPV of that surplus is roughly **£129,000**.

> So the money is real. It is just a third of what the headline suggests, it costs 20 extra
> hours a week, and it is conditional on lasting five years in a job the downside of which
> Alex has already named.

### What the model cannot price
Elster (2007, pp. 206–209) concedes that the optimal investment in information gathering is
**indeterminate**. Here, several decisive quantities are unknowable in advance:

- The probability MW grants the ACA exception (*"likely"*, not certain)
- The probability he stays five years in a 60-hour role he has not tried
- The value of optionality forgone by specialising at 21
- Whether a comparable offer exists in eight months

IRC can tell him to weigh these. It cannot tell him what they are.

## 5. 🚨 The question I cannot answer for you

**The assessment requires a decision you *made*.** You have described an offer, a constraint
and a dilemma. You have not said what you chose.

This matters because the deviation sits in a different place depending on the answer:

| If you… | The likely deviation | Component |
|---|---|---|
| **Turned MW down and stayed** | Sunk cost: the degree money and effort weighed as a reason, when IRC says they are spent either way | Action / consequentialism |
| **Accepted MW** | Anchoring on the headline £120,000 without testing it against tax, hours, or the ACA condition | Evidence → Expectations |
| **Have not decided yet** | ❌ Does not meet the brief — it must be a decision already made | — |

**Three things I need:**

1. **What did you decide, and when?**
2. **Did you run any of these numbers before deciding, or after?** (If after, that is itself
   the finding.)
3. **Was there a moment the decision felt settled** — a conversation, a form, a reply sent?
   A decision needs a point in time.

Answer those and the piece writes quickly. The analysis above gives it far more material
than the iPhone ever could.
