## ⚠️ v11 corrects a logic error, and finishes two jobs v10 left half-done

### The logic error

v10 implied that **wanting the job** steered the search. That is not what happened. Alex
**declined** the job. The £80,000 figure he believed argued *for* accepting, so it cannot
also explain withdrawing.

The accurate account is sharper, and it names **four** broken links instead of one:

| | Link | What went wrong |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Evidence → Expectations | Search stopped at the headline, so the belief was wrong by half |
| 2 | **Expectations → Action** | The belief argued *for* the job. He withdrew anyway, so the action did not follow from it either |
| 3 | **nothing → Action** | What actually moved him was the tuition already spent, which the model has no box for |
| 4 | Action, option set | A third option never generated |

Link 2 is the one v10 missed entirely, and it is the strongest: **his own stated belief
pointed the other way.**

### Job one: the parts now visibly connect
> *"So I withdrew holding a figure that argued for the opposite, and never tested it. What
> moved me instead was three years of tuition already paid."*

Two sentences, three components, and the substitution of one input for another.

### Job two: the critique is now about the model, not about him
v10 said *"Hargreaves Heap explains why I never looked"* — which explains **Alex**. A
criticism of the model has to be a claim about the model:

> *"The model is weaker here than it looks. It demands the right amount of searching without
> saying what that is."*

Eight words, then the citation. That is the difference between describing a limitation and
merely illustrating one.

## ✂️ What changed in v9: the lecturer's wording is out

v8 contained phrasing lifted close to the slides, most obviously *"Rational decision-makers
do not have 'I didn't think of that' moments"*. That is the lecturer's sentence, not yours,
and handing it back reads as recall rather than understanding.

| v8 (lifted) | v9 (yours) |
|---|---|
| *"Rational decision-makers do not have 'I didn't think of that' moments."* | *"The possibility occurred to me when my parents raised it, after I had withdrawn."* |
| *"Action requires listing every option."* | *"The model asks for every option on the table."* |
| *"preferences cannot themselves be irrational"* | *"Elster (2007) treats preferences as given, so wanting the money was not the error."* |

The framework is unchanged. Only the voice is. The concrete version of the deferral point is
also **stronger** than the aphorism it replaced, because it shows the failure instead of
naming it.

**Quotations from the published readings stay**, because citing Elster and Hargreaves Heap is
correct practice. Lifting a lecturer's slide is not the same thing.

## What changed from v7

The deferral answer confirmed a third deviation, and the audit surfaced three unused items
from the lecturer's own slides.

### ① The Action deviation is now in, in the lecturer's words
L1 slide 23: *"Rational decision-makers never have 'I didn't think of that!' moments.
Violations of the IRC model at this step often involve unconscious restriction of available
possible choices."*

You had the hiring manager on the phone and never asked about deferral. The option surfaced
only when your parents raised it. That is the slide, verbatim, happening to you.

### ② Your parents are *evidence*
L1 slide 29 lists **"advice from others"** as evidence a rational agent uses. So the evidence
existed and arrived — just **after** the action. That is a timing failure in the
Evidence→Action path, not an absence of information.

### ③ Expectations and Evidence are now one paragraph
Sanctioned by L2 slide 5: *"Often the relationship between expectations and evidence is
important, might be worth combining them."* It saves words and puts the false belief directly
beside the search that failed to correct it.

## ⚠️ The hindsight-bias defence — do not cut this line

L3 slide 16 warns: *"Beware of hindsight bias."* A sharp marker will ask whether £37,898 is a
number you only have now.

> *"The arithmetic took twenty minutes and I am a trainee accountant: the evidence was not
> unavailable, it was uncollected."*

That sentence is the answer. The information existed at the time; you did not look. It
converts the objection into the violation. Same for deferral: your parents did not invent the
option, they noticed it.
