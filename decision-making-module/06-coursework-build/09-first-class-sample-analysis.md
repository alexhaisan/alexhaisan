# 09 — The First-Class Sample: Language and Flow

**Source:** `sample_first_class.docx`. Declared word count 300; measured body 295.

> **This changes the format decision.** It is worth reading before anything else in this
> folder.

---

## 1. The format finding

It is **not** in the labelled-heading format of the four Lecture 2 exemplars. It is
**continuous prose**: a short header block, then six paragraphs, no bullet points, no
sub-headings.

```
The Psychology of Economic and Business Decisions (BUSI2051)
Instrumental Rational Choice application exercise
Word Count: 300

¶1  Orientation: the decision, named, plus what the model will be used for
¶2  Action
¶3  Preferences
¶4  Expectations
¶5  Evidence
¶6  Conclusion
```

**The signposting trick.** It gets the benefit of labelled sections without using them,
by naming the component in the **first sentence of each paragraph**:

> *"According to the IRC model, **action** involves choosing between all available options."*
> *"My **preferences** included comfort and durability…"*
> *"However, the **expectations** I formed were irrational."*
> *"The **evidence**, such as customer reviews and TikTok videos, revealed…"*

The marker never has to hunt, and the prose still flows. That is the move to copy.

### It declares its word count at the top
Three lines of header: module, assessment title, word count. Cheap, and it signals you
respected the limit.

### ⚠️ This corrects my earlier advice

`07-prose-and-style.md` told you to target a spread of roughly 5 to 30 words. **That is wider
than a first-class answer in this genre actually uses.** Real range here is 7 to 23, with a
standard deviation of 3.6. This is steady academic prose, not blog prose.

What the variation does is **purposeful rather than decorative**. There is exactly **one**
short sentence in the whole piece:

> *"However, the expectations I formed were irrational."* — 7 words

It sits at the pivot, opening the paragraph where the argument turns from "this was rational"
to "this was not". One short sentence, placed where the argument turns. That is the technique.

**Revised target: mean around 16, most sentences between 12 and 23, one or two short
sentences placed at genuine turns.** Not four.

## 3. The engine: a four-beat pattern, repeated

Every analytical paragraph runs the same sequence:

1. **State what IRC requires**
2. **State what I actually did**
3. **Name the violation**
4. *(sometimes)* **Draw the implication**

> *"In the IRC model, expectations should be derived solely from evidence and remain
> independent of preferences. This belief showed my **wishful thinking**, allowing what I
> wished to be true to influence what I believed to be true. This **exposes a weakness of the
> IRC model**…"*

Requirement, behaviour, violation, implication. Four beats, tight.

It is repetitive in structure and that is a **strength** here. The marker is checking whether
each component was understood and applied. A repeated structure makes that visible four times
over. Repetition of *structure* is not the same as repetition of *phrasing*.

## 4. Six language moves worth stealing

### ① The "However" pivot
Paragraph 4 opens with it, and that single word restructures the whole piece. Everything
before is conceded as rational. Everything after is the deviation.

### ② Concede before you attack
> *"Elster (2007) argues that preferences themselves cannot be irrational… Therefore, if they
> maximise my utility, choosing the Adidas shoe could be considered rational."*

An entire paragraph spent establishing that one component was **fine**. That is not wasted
space. It demonstrates understanding of the component, which is Trait 1, and it sets up the
contrast.

### ③ Name a weakness of the model, not just of yourself
> *"This exposes a weakness of the IRC model, which suggests beliefs form independently of
> preferences and therefore fails to display real human behaviour."*

**This is the Trait 2 unlock**, and it confirms the move already made in our v4. A first-class
answer does not stop at "I was irrational". It says what that reveals about IRC.

### ④ State the interactions in the conclusion
> *"My preferences distorted **both** my expectations **and** perception of evidence."*

Two interactions, one sentence, at the end. Preferences → Expectations and
Preferences → Evidence. Exactly what L3 slide 17 asks for.

### ⑤ Use the module's vocabulary exactly
*wishful thinking*, *utility*, *evidence*, *expectations*. No elegant variation. The component
names never shift.

### ⑥ One concrete, contemporary, personal detail
> *"customer reviews and **TikTok videos**"*

Specific, dated, unmistakably theirs. That is Trait 2's *"strong evidence of observation"* in
three words.

## 5. Where it is weak, which is useful calibration

A first was awarded to a piece with real flaws. Worth knowing.

| Weakness | Detail |
|---|---|
| **The Action paragraph never finds a violation** | It states that action involves listing all options, then discusses only two pairs of shoes. The option set is never examined for restriction. A whole component is described rather than analysed. |
| **A logical slip in Preferences** | *"if they maximise my utility, choosing the Adidas shoe could be considered rational"* — preferences being admissible does not make the **choice** rational, since the choice also depends on beliefs, which the next paragraph says were distorted. |
| **The Evidence paragraph is thin** | 34 words, and the mechanism is asserted rather than shown: *"I focused on positive reviews"*. No account of why. |
| **No reference list** | Elster (2007) is cited in text with no full reference. Strictly incomplete Harvard. |
| **One awkward phrase** | *"fails to display real human behaviour"* — "display" should be "describe" or "predict". |

**So the bar is reachable.** You do not need a flawless piece. You need the four components
present, at least one genuine violation diagnosed, a named weakness of the model, and the
interactions stated.

expectation acted against is a better finding than wishful thinking, which is the obvious
answer most students will reach for.

**On format and length we were behind.** Labelled headings read as notes beside this, and we
were leaving 50 words of the allowance unused.

---

## 7. What changes, and why

1. **Switch to flowing prose.** Six paragraphs, component named in each opening sentence.
   Both formats are permitted, but the first-class example flows, and prose carries
   *interactions* better than boxes do.
2. **Use the allowance.** 283 words rather than 248. The extra goes to the Action paragraph,
   which is where we are strongest and the sample is weakest.
3. **Add the header block**, including the declared word count.
4. **Calm the rhythm.** Target mean ~16, range 7-23, with short sentences only at genuine
   pivots.
5. **Keep everything v4 gained.** The sourced critique of IRC and both interactions stay.
