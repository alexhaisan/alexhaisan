# 08 — The Scaffold

> **The single page to write from.** Every slot specified: what goes in it, how many words,
> what shape the sentences should take, and what the marker is looking for there.
>
> Format confirmed by the four exemplars. Word weights set against their actual lengths
> (166–258). Craft notes from [`07-prose-and-style.md`](07-prose-and-style.md).

---

## Budget at a glance

| Slot | Words | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| Decision | 25–30 | Context |
| Actions Available | 30–35 | **Criterion 1** — and the unconsidered options |
| Preferences | 40–45 | **Criterion 1** — the conflict and trade-off |
| Beliefs / Evidence | 55–65 | **Criterion 1** — and the raw material for the interaction |
| Conclusion | 85–95 | **Criterion 2** — counterfactual, component, mechanism, interaction |
| | **≈ 245** | Leaves ~55 spare under the 300 ceiling |

Deliberately under. A tight piece reads better than a padded one, and the slack absorbs a
citation if you use one.

---

## Slot 1 — Decision · 25–30 words

**Goes in:** what you chose, when, and the figure if there is one. Nothing else.

**Shape:** one sentence, possibly two. Open with the fact, not with scene-setting.

> ✅ *"On 3 October I let a £14.99 monthly subscription renew, having opened the cancellation
> page and closed it again."*
> ❌ *"Recently, I found myself in a situation where I had to make a difficult decision
> regarding a subscription service that I had been using."*

**Avoid:** "Recently", "I found myself", any sentence that could precede any decision.
Specificity is free credibility.

---

## Slot 2 — Actions Available · 30–35 words

**Goes in:** the options you considered, **then the ones you did not**.

**Shape:** two sentences. First the framing you used, second what you left out. The second
sentence is the one none of the four exemplars writes.

> *"I treated this as a choice between renewing and cancelling. Two further options were
> available and did not occur to me: the £5.99 tier, and the three-month pause in the account
> settings."*

**Why the second sentence matters:** L3 slide 16 — *"look for decisions where you have
restricted choices too early… where you didn't consider a good potential choice."* It also
sets up an Action-component deviation without you having to argue for it later.

---

## Slot 3 — Preferences · 40–45 words

**Goes in:** what you wanted, **and what you wanted to avoid** (L2 slide 4). Flat list. At
least two that pull against each other.

**Shape:** one sentence listing, or a short list. Do not rank them.

> *"I wanted to reduce my monthly outgoings, to keep access to two series I was partway
> through, and to avoid the small friction of re-subscribing if I changed my mind."*

**Trap — L2 slide 5:** *"Avoid privileging some preferences over others. 'I went against my
preferences' is not a compelling argument."*

> ❌ *"I really wanted to save money but gave in to wanting the convenience."*
> ✅ *"These pulled in opposite directions, and the trade-off was never made explicitly."*

Three preferences is natural here. **Don't force a third if you only have two** — the rule of
three is a known tell.

---

## Slot 4 — Beliefs / Evidence · 55–65 words

**Goes in:** what you expected each option to produce, then what that expectation rested on.
Combining the two headings is sanctioned by L2 slide 5.

**Shape:** state the belief. State the evidence. **Then state the gap between them.** That
third move is what makes the Conclusion's interaction claim possible.

> *"I believed I would watch more over winter than I had over summer. The evidence I held was
> my own viewing history: four hours across four months. It did not support that belief. What
> did support it was a vague sense that there is always something worth watching, which comes
> from the platform's recommendations rather than from my use of them."*

**Trap — L3 slide 16:** *"Beware of hindsight bias."* Say what the evidence supported **at
the time**, not what you now know. Elster (2007, p. 209): consequences cannot explain a
decision, and the later self is an external observer.

---

## Slot 5 — Conclusion · 85–95 words

**The only slot with real prose. Four moves, in order.**

### Move 1 — the counterfactual · ~20 words
> *"An instrumentally rational agent with these preferences cancels, or drops to the cheaper tier."*

Say *"IRC would predict"* or *"An instrumentally rational agent would"*. Explicit. All four
exemplars do this and it is the task sentence's core requirement.

### Move 2 — what you did · 2–6 words
> *"I renewed."*

**Give it its own sentence.** It is the hinge, and the length contrast does the emphasis for
free.

### Move 3 — the component, with the mechanism · ~30 words
> *"The largest gap sits at Action: I had framed a two-way choice when four options were
> live, and no comparison of expected utilities can recover an option that was never in the
> set."*

**One** component (L2 slide 6 asks which gap is *biggest*). Name the mechanism, don't just
assert irrationality.

### Move 4 — the interaction · ~25 words
> *"My beliefs were accurate and my evidence sufficient; what failed was the link between
> wanting the service and never testing whether a cheaper version would do."*

**This is the highest-value sentence in the piece.** L3 slide 17: *"to what extent have they
used components **and interactions between them**."* Pick one link and say what went wrong
across it.

### Optional move 5 — evaluation · ~15 words, only if under budget
> *"The model is not thereby refuted; it is the benchmark that makes the gap visible."*

### Rhythm check
Target spread across the five sentences: roughly **20 / 3 / 30 / 25 / 12**. If every sentence
lands between 15 and 25, break one up.

## Final pass — in this order

1. **Content.** Four components mapped? One named as biggest? One interaction stated?
2. **Category check.** Is each row the *kind* of thing that component is?
   Action = options · Preferences = wants · Beliefs = expectations · Evidence = basis
3. **Count.** Under 300. Aim 245–270.
4. **Rhythm.** Write down each Conclusion sentence's length. Spread at least 5–30?
5. **Style.** [`07-prose-and-style.md`](07-prose-and-style.md) checklist. No em dashes, no
   tells, -ise spellings.
6. **Read aloud.** Whole thing, slowly.
7. **Proof.** Errors only. Do not let a tool restyle your sentences.
8. **Format, appendix, submit.** Before 3pm on 26 October.
