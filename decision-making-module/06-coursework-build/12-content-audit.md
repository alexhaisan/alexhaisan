### 🔑 The finding

**The Elster four-component diagram appears in every single lecture** — four times in
Lecture 1 alone, then opening Lectures 2, 3 and 4. It is the module's signature visual.

Its exact layout, from the slide geometry:

```
                    ACTION
                   ↗        ↖
         PREFERENCES        EXPECTATIONS
                   ↘            ↑ ↓
                            EVIDENCE
```

Four links: Preferences→Action, Expectations→Action, Preferences→Evidence,
Evidence⇄Expectations. Note the lecturer's version **omits** the crossed-out
Preferences→Expectations line from Elster's book and the information self-loop.

**Answer 2 reproduced this diagram with their own decision mapped onto it and the failing
links in red.** That is the single best idea in either sample, and it is now
`11-figures/figure-1-irc-components.svg`.

## ⚠️ Part 3 — The hindsight-bias risk, and how the draft defends against it

**L3 slide 16 warns: *"Beware of hindsight bias."*** A sharp marker will ask whether
£37,898 is a figure you only have *now*, used to condemn a decision made *then*. If so, the
whole argument collapses.

**It is not, and the draft says why.** The distinction:

| | Hindsight bias | This case |
|---|---|---|
| Information | Became available only afterwards | Was available at the time |
| Fault | None — you could not have known | **Condition (iii)** — you did not look |

The defending line is:

> *"The arithmetic took twenty minutes and I am a trainee accountant: the evidence was not
> unavailable, it was uncollected."*

That sentence is load-bearing. **Do not cut it.** It converts a potential objection into the
violation itself.

> The deferral point passes the same test. The option existed throughout; your parents did
> not create it, they noticed it.

## Part 4 — What changed as a result

Three additions, all from the lecturer's own words:

1. **L1 s23** → *"Rational decision-makers do not have 'I didn't think of that' moments."*
   Using the lecturer's phrasing back, for a case that matches it exactly.
2. **L1 s29** → advice from others counts as evidence. Your parents raised deferral, which
   means the evidence arrived, just **after** the action.
3. **L3 s16** → the Action violation is now named as the lecturer frames it: a good option
   not considered.

Two deliberate omissions, recorded so they are choices rather than oversights:
- ***Homo economicus*** would add register but no content.
- The **five-step procedure** would structure the piece but cost 40 words the analysis needs.
