## 1. What it does

**Format: labelled, telegraphic, with visual aids.** Not flowing prose, and not quite the
Lecture 2 exemplar style either. Headings, near-bullets, compressed grammar, plus **a figure
and a table**.

```
Header block: student ID, title, Word count: 299
Decision          one sentence
Action            the four options, plus the irreversibility constraint
Preferences       three bullets
Evidence          broken down per preference: Money / Career / Friends / Other / Search
[Figure 1]        monthly pay rise vs extra rent in London
Expectations      narrowing the field
[Table]           Preference × Bristol × London
IRC Prediction    its own heading
Resulting Decision and why it deviated
  Evidence gathering
  Evidence vs expectation
  Preference vs expectations
```

That last block is the clever part. See §3.

## 3. Four things it does better than us

### ① The scenario is genuinely complex

Career, geography, money, friendships, four live options, a **four-month** decision window,
and an **irreversible** submission. Both rubric traits reserve 70+ partly for *"complex
decision-making scenarios"*.

**A phone purchase cannot compete on this axis, and no amount of writing closes the gap.**
This is the one structural disadvantage we cannot engineer away.

### ② It labels its interactions

The strongest device in the whole document:

> **Evidence gathering** — *"despite a 4-month decision period and irreversible choice I did
> not cost the move"*
> **Evidence vs expectation** — *"I anchored on the London plan and £3000 salary gain,
> dismissing my Bristol colleagues as ill advised"*
> **Preference vs expectations** — *"Prestige of London office inflated London's merits and
> hid Bristol's merits (wishful thinking)"*

Three interactions, each given its own label. L3 slide 17 asks *"to what extent have they
used components **and interactions between them**"*, and this answer makes them impossible to
miss. Ours are stated but woven into prose.

### ③ It quantifies, and the arithmetic does real work

> *"London pays £3000 more than other offices, but after tax, NI and additional rent (£1100
> vs £900), this is cancelled out."*

That is proper economics: gross to net, plus the differential cost of living, producing a
conclusion that reverses the naive reading. Plus a figure plotting pay rise against extra
rent. Numeracy is a stated module outcome and this evidences it directly.

### ④ One genuinely original observation

> *"Anxiety of the choice made finishing the form the goal and relief on submission."*

The goal displaced from *making the right decision* to *completing the task*. Nothing in the
taught material covers that, and it is the kind of observation that reads as real.

## 4. Four things we do better

### ① It never criticises IRC

Searched end to end: **there is no critique of the model anywhere.** It diagnoses the
student's failure thoroughly and stops. Trait 2's 70-79 band opens with *"thorough
comprehension of **critiques of IRC**"*, and this does not clear that gate.

Our v5 does, sourced to Elster's own admission that the optimal investment in information
gathering is indeterminate (2007, pp. 206-209).

### ② No citation, no reference
Not one, anywhere. The format spec mandates Harvard.

### ③ The prose is hard to read
> *"Evidence gathering despite a 4-month decision period and irreversible choice I did not
> cost the move."*

Missing punctuation, missing connective. Compression has gone past terse into unclear. A
marker assessing *"thorough comprehension"* has to work to extract the argument.

### ④ Our Expectations finding is analytically harder
Answer 2: false beliefs from *"information asymmetry"*. The first-class sample: wishful
thinking. **Both are belief failures, which is the obvious answer.**

Ours: both expectations were **accurate**, and the action did not follow. That is Elster's
requirement (i) rather than (ii), and it is rarer.

### Also: an unfinished table
The *Saving Money* row of its comparison table is **empty**. Either unfinished or the content
sat in an image. Either way it reads as incomplete.

## 6. What we take from it

### ✅ Take: quantify the trade-off properly

A2's tax-NI-rent calculation is the best single thing in it. We have the raw material for an
equivalent and have not used it:

- iPhone 17 Pro: **£1,099**
- Battery repair: **£150**
- Premium paid: **£949**
- Expected duration of the status benefit, by Alex's own estimate: **about one week**

**≈£135 per day.** That number is pure arithmetic from facts already in the draft, it makes
the expected-utility comparison concrete, and it lands exactly where the argument turns.

### ✅ Take: make the interactions unmissable
We have two, woven into sentences. A2 labels three. In prose we cannot use sub-headings
without breaking the format, but we can sharpen the signalling.

### ⚠️ Consider: a figure or small table
Permitted by the brief (*"bullet-points, diagrams etc."*), and A2 uses both. But the
first-class sample uses neither and still scored a first, and figure text probably counts
toward the 300. **Not worth it at our word count.**

### ❌ Do not take: the telegraphic style
It costs more in clarity than it buys in content, and the first-class sample proves full
sentences are not a handicap.

## 7. One open question for Alex

Answer 2 leans hard on **irreversibility**: *"Once submitted, a request can't be withdrawn."*
That makes its search failure far worse, since the student had four months and one shot.

**The iPhone purchase was reversible.** Apple's standard return window is 14 days, and the
novelty faded inside one. So the evidence that would have overturned the decision arrived
**while the decision was still reversible**, and it was not acted on.

That is potentially a second, sharper failure, and it is the sort of observation that reads as
genuinely yours. But it only works if it is true to what happened.

**Did you know the return window was open, and did you consider returning it once the novelty
went?** If yes, it goes in. If the thought never occurred, that is itself an Action-component
point worth a clause. If you would rather not claim either, we leave it out.
