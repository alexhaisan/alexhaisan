## 3. The four theories most likely to be on a module like this

If the module covers behavioural economics at all, it almost certainly covers these. In
rough order of likelihood:

1. **Bounded rationality / satisficing** — Simon (1955). The foundational critique.
2. **Heuristics and biases** — Tversky & Kahneman (1974). The empirical programme.
3. **Prospect theory** — Kahneman & Tversky (1979). The alternative formal model.
4. **Hyperbolic discounting / present bias** — Laibson (1997); O'Donoghue & Rabin (1999).

A 300-word answer only has room for **one**. Choose the one that fits your decision most
precisely, not the one that sounds most impressive.

## 4. Choosing well — three criteria

| Criterion | Test |
|---|---|
| **Taught** | Does it appear in the module slides/reading list? If no → reject. |
| **Precise fit** | Does the theory's *mechanism* explain *why* you did what you did — or does it just relabel it? If it only relabels, reject. |
| **Citable** | Can you cite the primary source in Harvard, in-text, in ~8 words? |

**The relabelling trap.** Writing *"I was subject to present bias"* explains nothing; it just
names the behaviour in Greek. Writing *"my discount function is hyperbolic rather than
exponential, so the near-term reward was weighted disproportionately at the moment of choice,
producing a preference reversal relative to the plan I had made a week earlier"* explains the
mechanism. At 300 words you have room for one such sentence. Make it that one.
