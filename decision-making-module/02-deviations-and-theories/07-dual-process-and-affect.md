## Dual process theory (Stanovich & West, 2000; Kahneman, 2011)

| | **System 1** | **System 2** |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Fast | Slow |
| Effort | Automatic, effortless | Controlled, effortful |
| Awareness | Unconscious | Conscious |
| Capacity | Massively parallel | Serial, capacity-limited |
| Control | Involuntary | Voluntary |
| Output | Impressions, intuitions, feelings | Judgements, reasoned conclusions |
| Error profile | Systematic biases | Fatigue, laziness, "cognitive miser" |

**The key claim:** System 1 generates an answer *continuously and automatically*; System 2
is supposed to monitor and override it but is **lazy** and often endorses the intuition
without checking.

> **Attribute substitution** (Kahneman & Frederick, 2002): faced with a hard question
> ("what is the probability?"), System 1 silently answers an easier one ("how similar is
> it?" / "how easily does an example come to mind?"). The substitution is *invisible to the
> agent*. This is the engine underneath the whole heuristics-and-biases programme.

### Cognitive Reflection Test (Frederick, 2005)
> *A bat and ball cost £1.10 in total. The bat costs £1.00 more than the ball. How much does
> the ball cost?*

System 1 answers 10p — fast, confident, wrong (the correct answer is 5p). Over 50% of MIT,
Harvard and Princeton students gave the intuitive answer. The point is not that the
question is hard; it is that the wrong answer arrives *before* you have decided to think.

### Conditions that disable System 2
Cognitive load; time pressure; fatigue; alcohol; stress; ego depletion; **scarcity**
(Mullainathan & Shafir, 2013 — scarcity consumes "bandwidth", degrading decision quality in
*unrelated* domains).

## Visceral factors (Loewenstein, 1996)

Hunger, thirst, pain, fatigue, sexual arousal, craving, anger and fear **temporarily and
dramatically** reorder preferences toward the goods that relieve them.

- **Hot–cold empathy gap:** in a cold (calm) state you systematically **underestimate** how
  strongly a hot state will drive your behaviour — and vice versa. You cannot simulate the
  other state from inside the current one.
- **Projection bias** (Loewenstein, O'Donoghue & Rabin, 2003): you project the current state
  onto your future self — shopping hungry, buying a convertible on a sunny day.

> The hot–cold empathy gap is a **second-order** failure and worth noting: the problem is not
> only that you act badly in the hot state, but that in the cold state you *do not plan for*
> acting badly. That is precisely why commitment devices, rather than resolutions, are the
> right response — which links straight into component D.

## Affect heuristic (Slovic et al., 2002)
Risk and benefit are judged by **how you feel** about something. In reality risk and benefit
are usually *positively* correlated; in judgement they are *negatively* correlated, because
a single affective impression drives both.

## Using it in the coursework

**Fits if:** the decision was made fast, under pressure, tired, emotional, hungry, angry or
stressed; you "just knew" and didn't check; you later could not reconstruct a reason;
the intuitive answer arrived before any deliberation.

**Careful:** dual-process is a *description of the mechanism*, and on its own can read as
vague. It works best **paired with a specific bias** — "System 2 failed to override an
availability-driven System 1 estimate" is much stronger than "I used System 1."

**Specimen sentence (≈35 words):**
> *Under time pressure, System 2 failed to monitor and override the intuitive response
> (Kahneman, 2011): I substituted an easier attribute for the probability judgement the
> decision actually required, and acted on the substitution without detecting it.*

## Future-application ideas (component D)

- **Insert a mandatory delay** between option and action for decisions above a set value —
  a cooling-off rule that structurally gives System 2 time to engage.
- **Decide in the cold state, execute in the hot state** — shopping lists, pre-set limits,
  pre-booked commitments.
- **Checklists** (Gawande, 2009) — forcing explicit verification of steps System 1 skips.
  Strong evidence base in medicine and aviation.
- **Never decide while hungry, tired or angry** — a bright-line rule, cheap to apply.
- **Write the reasoning down before acting.** Writing recruits System 2 and leaves an audit
  trail you can review later for patterns.
