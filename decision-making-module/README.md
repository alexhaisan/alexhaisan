## The model, in brief

**Instrumental** Rational Choice: an action is rational when it is the best **means** to the
agent's own ends, given their beliefs. Reason serves ends it does not itself evaluate —
*"Reason is slave to the passions"* (Hume, quoted slide 24).

**Elster's four components** (lecture slide 16; Elster's own labels in brackets):

| Component | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Action** | A choice between alternative courses of action |
| **Preferences** *(desires)* | Goals, desires, aims, wants — which give utility |
| **Expectations** *(beliefs)* | Probabilities, beliefs |
| **Evidence** *(information)* | Information, data |

**Elster's three optimality requirements** (2007, p. 191): the **action** must be optimal
given the beliefs; the **beliefs** must be as well supported as possible given the evidence;
the **evidence** must result from an optimal investment in information gathering.

**The blocked arrow.** Preferences must not flow *directly* into expectations — that is
**wishful thinking**. They may flow into expectations *through* evidence-gathering. Elster:
the wish must not be *"the father of the thought"*, but desires can be the
***grandfather*** of the beliefs.

## The three ideas that run through everything

### 1. Preferences cannot be irrational
Slide 25, with the lecturer's own warning attached: *"Watch out for confusion about the word
'irrational' here!"* Elster is blunter — *"the confusion of rationality and egoism is a crude
error"* — and goes further: an agent *"under the influence of emotion or drugs"* who prefers
A to B **acts rationally in choosing A**.

So your deviation cannot be *what you wanted*. It must sit at one of the four component
sites: a restricted **option set**, an inconsistently resolved **preference trade-off**,
an **expectation** not supported by evidence, or non-optimal **evidence** search.

### 2. Outcome is not process
*"Consequences of a decision cannot explain it. Only the mental states that **precede** the
decision enable us to explain the action... rather than to characterize it as useful or
beneficial from the point of view of an external observer **or of the agent at a later
time**"* (Elster, p. 209).

"It turned out badly" and "I regret it now" are both the later self's view. Neither is a
deviation.

### 3. Doing the right thing by fluke isn't rational either
The arrows in Figure 11.1 carry **causality** as well as optimality: *"the action should be
**caused by** the beliefs that make it a rational one; it is not enough to do the right thing
by fluke"* (p. 191).
