## The three ideas that run through everything

### 1. The deviation must be procedural, not preferential
The brief asks what *"an instrumentally rational decision-maker **with your preferences**"*
would do. IRC constrains the **structure** of preferences and the **process** of acting on
them — never their **content**. So "I wanted the fun thing" is not a deviation; it is a
preference, and the model accommodates it.

Your deviation has to be a failure of **consistency**, **belief formation**, **means–ends
alignment**, or **search**. The **Deviation Test** in `01-core-theory/04-irc-worked-template.md`
screens for exactly this, and it is the first filter every candidate decision must pass.

### 2. Elster's three conditions localise the failure
Rational action requires that (i) the action best serves the desire given the belief,
(ii) the belief is the best the evidence supports, and (iii) the evidence gathered was
itself optimal. **Three separate places to break.** Naming *which one* broke is what turns
"I was irrational" into analysis.

### 3. Outcome is not process
A good decision can have a bad outcome. IRC judges the decision **at the moment of choosing,
on the evidence then available**. "It turned out badly" is the most common wrong answer to
this assessment.
